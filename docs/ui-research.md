# Build Lift Ledger around the one-tap set

Lift Ledger's customers are best served by a design built around speed and trust rather than a particular look. Each set should take one tap because last session's numbers are already filled in. Home should open on "Continue: Day 2". A new user should log a first set seconds after launch, with no account and no setup. A shared program should show a preview before import and open from WhatsApp. Arabic should be properly mirrored, with Western digits isolated so "62.5 kg" and "3×10" never scramble. The category leaders prove the point. Strong and Hevy each hold **4.9★ from more than 100K App Store ratings** and win praise for fast logging. The loudest complaints across the category are paywalls on core features, "too many taps to log a set", social clutter and navigation that locks users inside an active workout. Retention data explains the stakes. Health and fitness apps keep roughly **27% of users after day 1 and 3–8% by day 30**. In one 2026 study, training sessions fell **69% between week 1 and the end of month 1**, and the median user waited **about 8 days** after signing up before training at all. The first session therefore decides most outcomes. Lift Ledger already avoids the biggest complaint class by being free, offline and account-free. Its real openings are a trainer-to-trainee link that previews and updates cleanly, and a free logger that is properly built for Arabic, a lane global apps and Arabic-branded apps have both left largely open. The strongest of the three visual directions below for this audience is a calm, dense "Ledger" system that uses one typeface family for Arabic and Latin, warmed with the supportive tone that has won recent design awards.

## Leaders win on taps per set and lose on paywalls and clutter

The strength-app market splits into three archetypes, and Lift Ledger sits between two of them. Blank-log loggers (Strong, Hevy) optimise for logging speed. Program followers (Boostcamp, Liftosaur) hand you a ready-made program that progresses. AI planners (Fitbod) decide the workout for you. Hevy is the growth story. It claims **18M+ users** and calls its philosophy "hands-off", providing tools without prescribing how people train ([Hevy About Us](https://www.hevyapp.com/about-us/)). It keeps a free tier with no ads and charges $23.99/year for Pro ([App Store: Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458862350)). Strong caps free users at **3 custom routines** ([App Store: Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)), and a reviewer calls that "a major restriction for four- or five-day splits" ([RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-review/)). Boostcamp offers 132 coach programs and 12,000+ user programs but puts most analytics behind a $59.99/year Pro tier ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/boostcamp-review)). Fitbod costs $79.99–95.99/year ([App Store: Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)). Lift Ledger mixes "continue my program", "pick a ready-made one" and "build my own". That makes it closest to Strong plus Boostcamp, with no routine cap and no paywall.

The set-logging screen has converged on a standard that Lift Ledger should adopt. Each exercise is a card holding a table of set rows. Hevy shows "your last session's weights and reps" on each exercise, starts rest timers automatically and tags warm-up, drop and failure sets ([App Store: Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458862350)). In Hevy you change a set's type by tapping its number ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/34953606698903-Build-a-Workout-Program-Create-Organize-Routines)). In Strong, last session's weight and reps "appear automatically", and a set takes about "three taps once you're in the session" ([RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-review/), a low-reliability site). Boostcamp lets users swap an exercise mid-workout and asks whether the swap applies **only today or to future workouts** ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/boostcamp-review)). That one question prevents silent program drift, and it maps directly onto Lift Ledger's existing swap feature. Hevy also added a warning for sets logged with 0 reps ([App Store: Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458862350)), a cheap guard against bad data. The usual column layout, `SET | PREVIOUS | KG | REPS | ✓` with grey "previous" values and a tick that tints the row, is widely familiar, but this research did not verify it from a primary source.

User sentiment comes with a caveat. The Reddit evidence arrived only second-hand, through a vendor blog that paraphrases threads. Its themes still line up with the App Store reviews. "Too many taps to log a set" is the most frequent complaint. Strong is praised for being "just faster for actual gym sets" and for its lack of extras. FitNotes is praised for being "completely free, no subscription, no upsell" ([Cora blog, vendor-written](https://www.corahealth.app/blog/best-workout-tracker-reddit)). Fitbod reviewers complain that "navigation is locked during an active workout; you must end the session to access the rest of the app" ([App Store: Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)). Taken together, Lift Ledger's free, offline model has already won the argument about monetisation. Its remaining design risks are taps per set, clutter on the workout screen, being trapped inside an active session, rigid swaps and a plate calculator that assumes one bar weight.

## Design the workout screen for a tired thumb at arm's length

Mid-workout use is distracted, one-handed and often sweaty, so platform minimums are a floor, not a target. Apple asks for **44×44 pt**, Material for **48×48 dp**, and WCAG 2.2 AA sets a legal floor of **24×24 CSS px**. The closest published analogue to gym use is Google's in-car guidance, which requires **76×76 dp** targets for use while a car is moving ([TetraLogical](https://tetralogical.com/blog/2022/12/20/foundations-target-size/)). NN/g recommends steppers of at least about 1 cm × 1 cm ([NN/g, Input Steppers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/input-steppers/)). Hoober's reanalysis of tap data, including Henze et al.'s millions of taps, found that taps are most accurate in the centre of the screen and "much worse" along the top and bottom edges. Centre targets can sit 7 mm apart, while top and bottom targets need **10–12 mm** spacing ([Hoober, UXmatters](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/11/design-for-fingers-and-thumbs-instead-of-touch.php)). The practical rule for Lift Ledger follows. The weight field, the steppers and the "set done" tick should be roughly **56–76 px tall** and sit in the middle-to-lower-middle of the screen. Delete and reset should be well away from them. The current set's weight should be the largest number on screen.

Weight entry should combine a typeable field with steppers. NN/g recommends steppers for values "with a clear, most-frequently-selected value" that users nudge slightly, and says the field should stay typeable for larger jumps, which are "tedious" one press at a time ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/input-steppers/)). Progressive overload is exactly that case: last week's 60 kg, plus or minus 2.5. In Baymard's testing, numeric keyboards "produced noticeably fewer typos and form validation errors", and their keys have a far larger hit area. Mixing keyboard types across fields confused test participants ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-touch-keyboards)). Practitioners warn against `type="number"` because of its spinner, scroll-wheel and locale quirks ([Valkhof](https://kilianvalkhof.com/2022/css-html/are-you-sure-thats-a-number-input/)). Weight should therefore use `type="text" inputmode="decimal"` and reps `inputmode="numeric"`. The field should accept both "." and "," as the decimal separator, normalise Arabic-Indic digits (٠–٩) to ASCII, and stay at 16 px or larger to avoid iOS zoom on focus. Pages should never autofocus on load. When the keyboard opens, the active set row should scroll to the centre of the visible area. With last session's values prefilled, ±2.5 kg steppers handle most sets without opening the keyboard at all. That matters because the keyboard covers half the screen.

The iOS web platform rules out several native habits, so set feedback has to be visual first. iOS web apps have **no Vibration API** and no Background Sync or Background Fetch. Web Push works only for installed home-screen apps (iOS 16.4+), and it needs a server, which an offline-first app does not have ([firt.dev iOS PWA Compatibility](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). A rest timer therefore cannot rely on haptics or background notifications. It should store a start timestamp and compute the time remaining, so it is still correct when the user comes back. A visible change should confirm every completed set: a check icon and a row state, never colour alone. An optional Web Audio cue can mark the end of rest, and Android can get `navigator.vibrate` as an extra. Screen Wake Lock is now available, including in installed iOS web apps **from iOS 18.4** ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Lift Ledger should hold the lock during an active session, re-acquire it when the page becomes visible again, and fail silently on older devices. Because history lives only on the device, it should also call `navigator.storage.persist()`, which iOS has supported since 15.2 ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)), to lower the risk of the browser deleting a user's history.

For screen readers, a row of bare numbers is meaningless. Each input needs an accessible name such as "Bench press, set 2, weight in kg". Each stepper should show its unit and step size and grey out at its limit ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/input-steppers/)). No research was found on sweaty-hand accuracy, on font sizes for reading at arm's length, or on rest-timer interfaces, so the sizes above are reasoned from the driving and stepper guidance rather than measured in gyms.

## The first session carries most of the retention weight

Fitness retention collapses quickly, which makes time to first value the most important onboarding metric. Adjust puts health and fitness **day-1 retention at 27%**. Across all categories it reports D1/D7/D30 of 26/13/7%, and it says only that MENA dips "slightly", without giving a figure ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/)). Business of Apps reports **3% day-30 retention** for health and fitness in 2023 ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)). The two use different methods, so treat 3–8% at day 30 as a range. A JMIR study of 2,771 users of a training app found sessions peaked in week 1 and then fell **69.3% by the end of month 1** and **80.6% by month 3**. The **median gap between registering and the first session was 7.98 days**, and users averaged **1.87 sessions a week** ([JMIR mHealth uHealth 2026](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e72201/PDF)). These benchmarks come from installed native apps, so a PWA's numbers will differ and be harder to measure. The direction still holds: anything between launch and the first logged set costs users.

The leaders do not exploit this opening. Hevy's onboarding runs through a feature carousel, account creation, a notification prompt, units, an Apple Health prompt, a **subscription paywall with yearly preselected**, a welcome tour and a "how did you hear about us" survey ([ScreensDesign: Hevy](https://screensdesign.com/apps/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/?vs=212387)). Boostcamp offers a quiz, a library or an AI-generated plan, and Garage Gym Reviews says its builder has "a small learning curve" ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/boostcamp-review)). Lift Ledger can beat them without any of that. First launch should ask one question (kg or lb), optionally followed by days per week and experience level. It should then recommend one ready-made 2–4 day program and land on Day 1 with a single obvious Start button, about three taps from first launch to a logged set. A trainee who opens a trainer's link should skip even that step and go straight to the program preview. Hevy teaches its tick convention once with a first-use "Got it!" modal ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/?vs=212387)). Lift Ledger should do the same inline on the first set: "Tap ✓ to log. Rest timer starts automatically."

What keeps users after the first session is mastery feedback, not guilt. In Barasch and Silverman's seven experiments with more than 4,000 participants, highlighting an intact streak raised continuation: **66.2% vs 57.9%** chose another strength exercise. Highlighting a broken streak *reduced* persistence, especially when people blamed themselves. The authors recommend not announcing breaks, counting weekly rather than daily, and allowing repair ([INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/marketing/consumer-streaks-are-motivating-key-keeping-them-alive)). The JMIR data agree. Intrinsic motivation correlated with retention (r=0.19), while guilt-based "introjected" motivation did not (r=0.07) ([JMIR](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e72201/PDF)). Woolley and Sharif's review warns that social comparison makes fitness users "feel inadequate and lose motivation". It favours less, more actionable feedback that rewards meaningful progress ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/332194)). Award juries reward the same thing. Gentler Streak won a 2024 Apple Design Award for reminders that are "optimistic and encouraging" and for "individual progression rather than comparison against others" ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/apple-announces-winners-of-the-2024-apple-design-awards)). Its team aims for copy that is "supportive but not cheesy" and lets users set a status of "sick, injured, or on a break" ([Sketch](https://sketch.com/blog/gentler-streak)).

For Lift Ledger this means a weekly adherence count ("2 of 3 days this week") instead of a daily streak, which also matches the observed rate of about 1.9 sessions a week. It also means PR moments and "beat last week" comparisons, no "streak lost" message, and a "taking a break" option. Ramadan deserves a specific version of that break. Gulf gyms schedule classes **two hours before and two hours after iftar**, with strength work concentrated after iftar ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2593812/saudi-arabia); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/ramadan/uae-fitness-why-golden-hour-before-iftar-is-busiest-time-for-workouts)). An Arabic-audience logger should therefore never assume morning training, and it should let users pause the weekly count without penalty.

## Treat a shared program like a PDF that can log itself

### Trainers still live in spreadsheets, PDFs and WhatsApp

Lift Ledger's sharing feature competes less with Hevy than with the PDF. In one r/personaltraining thread, a trainee describes Google Sheets that were hard to use on a phone in the gym and hid progress. A trainer mistook a five-month-old lift for a new PR. Another trainer with 27 clients builds Canva PDFs with a 12-week logging grid, refreshes them every 8–12 weeks, and calls the setup "extremely tedious" ([Reddit via mirror](https://redlib.hyperreal.coffee/r/personaltraining/comments/1jldg7n/sharing_workout_programs_with_clients)). A UAE coaching guide lists WhatsApp or Telegram, PDFs and apps side by side as delivery channels ([GetFitDXB](https://getfitdxb.pages.dev/blog/online-personal-trainer-dubai-guide), a low-authority site). An Iraqi coach's women-focused platform began as a WhatsApp group ([Al Bawaba, 2021](https://www.albawaba.com/editors-choice/iraqi-coach-establishes-online-fitness-platform-working-moms-1424641)). No hard data on regional delivery habits exists, but the pattern is consistent. The winning flow is as easy to send over WhatsApp as a PDF, can be logged like an app, and has no account, fee or username. Hevy Coach clients, by contrast, get random usernames that coaches cannot change ([Capterra: Hevy Coach](https://www.capterra.com/p/10015732/Hevy-Coach/reviews/)).

### Mobile builders are slow where they nest features behind menus

Consumer builders look short but hide their friction. Hevy's routine flow is three top-level steps, but a **superset takes 8 steps** through repeated "⋯" menus. Prescribing a rep target requires switching that exercise's reps field to "Rep Range" ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/34953606698903-Build-a-Workout-Program-Create-Organize-Routines)). The fast builders are keyboard tools. Boostcamp's free web creator combines "the efficiency of a spreadsheet" with lifting features, using Shift+Enter to add a set and drag to move days ([Boostcamp](https://boostcamp.app/program-creator)). Liftosaur parses text such as `Bench Press / 3x8-12` ([Liftosaur blog](https://www.liftosaur.com/blog/posts/launched-workout-planner)). TrueCoach links a superset with a single icon between two movements ([TrueCoach Help](https://help.truecoach.co/articles/3047972-the-workout-builder-basics)). Coaches consistently complain that building on mobile is weak: Hevy Coach reviewers in May, October and November 2026 say the mobile app forces them back to the web ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10015732/Hevy-Coach/reviews/)).

Lift Ledger's one-box-per-set builder is the right structure, because structured fields drive logging and progression. It needs four shortcuts to close the speed gap. The first is a quick-fill that turns "3×10" or "3×8–12" into filled boxes. The second is a multi-select exercise picker. The third is copy-forward for days and whole programs, since trainers keep beginner, intermediate and advanced templates. The fourth is a single "link with next" control for supersets. The library holds 171 exercises, few enough that a search bar plus muscle and equipment filter chips can show results instantly. This matches Hevy's search-plus-filter pattern across 400+ exercises ([Hevy Help: Exercise Library](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35688251991575-Hevy-Exercise-Library-400-Exercises-and-Custom-Exercises)). Custom exercises matter too, because coaches using big libraries still report having to create standard machines by hand ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10015732/Hevy-Coach/reviews/)).

### Preview before import, and make "update" a visible choice

On the trainee side, coaching platforms converge on a "Today" screen with one Start button. Everfit puts the day's workout at the top, shows a rest-day message when nothing is assigned, and opens an overview before the session starts ([Everfit Help](https://help.everfit.io/en/articles/3581190-client-app-today)). Hevy's shared links open a web page where anyone can view the routine without the app and then choose to save it. The sharer's weights are not copied: **the recipient's own history fills them** ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/34953501503895-How-to-Share-Workouts-and-Routines-Step-by-Step)). That is exactly Lift Ledger's "keep logged weights on update" behaviour. Hevy also shares folders as an **image** of each routine with sets × reps ([Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/share-folders-routines/)). That image travels well in WhatsApp and doubles as a trust preview. Update behaviour is where coaching tools diverge. TrueCoach gives each program a "Sync program changes" toggle, so clients either receive edits or keep the version they were given ([TrueCoach Help](https://help.truecoach.co/en/articles/4555142-sync-program-changes)). Hevy Coach pushes changes immediately, but reviewers complain that clients can edit and permanently save over a coach's program. They ask for locked programs that still allow temporary swaps ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10015732/Hevy-Coach/reviews/)). Trainerize users complain that week instructions don't appear when a session starts ([Capterra: Trainerize](https://www.capterra.com/p/140262/Trainerize/reviews/)).

Lift Ledger's link carries the whole program, which makes it unusual. Every competitor found uses a short server ID, such as liftosaur.com/p/d8fffaa ([Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/p/d8fffaa)). The encoded link has a cost and a hidden advantage. Long URLs make QR codes denser and WhatsApp previews uglier, so the share screen should lead with the system share sheet and offer QR and file as secondary options. The advantage is that the preview can render entirely offline, even from a QR scan, which no server-backed competitor can match. The import screen should show the program name, the trainer's name, the number of days, and each day's exercises with sets × reps, with one primary "Add this program" button. When a program with the same identity already exists, the screen should switch to "Update program — your logged weights are kept". It should offer "Add as a copy" as the alternative and list what changed. No fitness app documented in this research does that last step, so it is a recommendation rather than an observed pattern. Trainer notes should appear inside the workout itself, and a trainee's swap should default to "today only" so the trainer's prescription stays intact.

## Arabic support is an open lane if the digits behave

### The market gap is real but unmeasured

Global and regional products both leave room for a free, properly right-to-left logger. The top Saudi Android health and fitness apps in Q3 2025 were government health apps, an insurer and a gym chain, with no workout loggers ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q3-android-top-5-health%20and%20fitness-units-sa-600af518241bc16eb8dce802)). Arabic-branded apps often ship English interfaces. ArabFit has an Arabic subtitle but lists English as its only app language and charges in euros ([App Store: ArabFit](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/arabfit/id1572476371)). Fitness Storm calls itself "the first comprehensive Arabic platform", yet also lists English ([App Store: Fitness Storm](https://apps.apple.com/ae/app/fitness-storm/id6463420120)). WHOOP users have spent more than a year asking for Arabic ([WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/over-a-year-of-requests-still-no-arabic-support/15423.md)). Hevy lists Arabic among its 16 languages ([App Store: Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458862350)), which makes it the benchmark, although the quality of its RTL support was not assessed. No Iraq-specific rankings were found.

### Mirror the layout and progress, never the numbers or the checkmark

Apple's 2026 HIG and Google's older Material guidance agree on most mirroring rules. Back and next controls flip, and the back button points right. Controls that show progress from one value to another flip, as do calendars, timelines and ordered sequences. The numerals inside a number never reverse, and logos and "universal signs and marks" such as the **checkmark** stay unflipped ([Apple HIG: Right to left](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left.md)). Material adds that progress bars fill right to left, that clocks, clockwise progress circles and media controls stay as they are, and that "charts and graphs" are not mirrored ([Material Design m1](https://m1.material.io/usability/bidirectionality.html)). That Material guidance is older; the newer Material 3 page could not be read. The two sources conflict on one point that matters for Lift Ledger. Apple says to reverse chronological sequences, while Material says to leave charts alone. The defensible call is to keep a chart's time axis left to right, since digits run LTR too, while mirroring the titles, legends and chrome around it, and then test with Arabic users.

| Lift Ledger element | In RTL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Tab bar order, back/next chevrons, swipe-to-delete direction | Mirror | Apple, Material navigation rules |
| Horizontal rest-progress bar, week-progress bar | Mirror (fill from right) | Apple "flip progress controls"; Material |
| Circular rest-timer ring | Do not mirror | Material: clocks and clockwise circles stay |
| Checkmark on completed sets | Do not mirror | Apple: universal marks |
| Digits in "62.5", "3×10", "01:30" | Never reverse; isolate the token | Apple; W3C alreq |
| Progress-chart time axis | Keep LTR, mirror chrome (test) | Material vs Apple conflict |
| −/+ stepper positions | Undecided; test both | No source addresses steppers |
| Exercise photos/illustrations | Do not flip | Apple |

### Western digits are the right default, provided input tolerates both systems

Digit usage in the Arab world really is mixed. The Maghreb uses Western digits almost exclusively, and Saudi Arabia increasingly favours them. The UAE and Arabic-speaking Asia use both, "often employed alongside each other" ([Wikipedia: Eastern Arabic numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Arabic_numerals)). Apple tells number-centric apps to choose deliberately ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left.md)). The owner's choice of Western digits throughout fits that trend. It also has a technical benefit. Western digits are Unicode bidi class EN, while Arabic-Indic digits are AN, and "mixed strings in RTL text can display unexpectedly" ([W3C alreq](https://github.com/w3c/alreq/wiki/Arabic-numerals-%28Draft%29)). Western-only display removes one class of bidi surprise. Input must still accept everything Iraqi users might type. That means Arabic-Indic digits (U+0660–0669), Persian or Extended digits (U+06F0–06F9), and the Arabic decimal separator "٫" or a comma, all mapped to ASCII and ".". Display should be forced with `Intl.NumberFormat('ar', {numberingSystem: 'latn'})`. Every value-and-unit token should be wrapped in `<bdi>` or `unicode-bidi: isolate`, and "3×10" should stay a single isolated LTR span so it never reads as "10×3". A Samsung community request for Arabic-Indic digits with Arabic UI ([Samsung Community](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/Others/We-need-Arabic-Numbers-with-Arabic-Language/m-p/5774172)) suggests a future opt-in setting would find some users.

### Typography and terminology need one superfamily and real Arabic testers

Arabic has no uppercase, so Apple recommends setting Arabic **about 2 pt larger** when it sits next to all-caps Latin ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left.md)). In practice, uppercase "eyebrow" labels and positive letter-spacing, which breaks joined Arabic letterforms, need a weight-or-colour alternative in Arabic. Google's type team singles out Cairo as "highly legible as body text", with Latin and Arabic "neatly aligned", and warns that "trying to emulate calligraphy in type often backfires" ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/modernizing-arabic-typography-type-design/)). IBM Plex Sans Arabic is built to pair with Plex Sans Latin and is distributed openly ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/ibm-plex-sans-arabic/about)). It is the safest single family for an app where Arabic labels sit beside Western digits. Bundling one Arabic face is also worth the bytes, because Android's built-in Arabic fonts vary by manufacturer. Gym vocabulary is the weakest-evidenced area. App titles show formal "نادي رياضي" and "صالة رياضية" alongside the loanword "الجيم" ([ArabFit](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/arabfit/id1572476371)). That supports Lift Ledger's plan of Modern Standard Arabic names with English subtitles. No source verified the Arabic terms for set, rep, superset or PR, or Iraqi dialect usage, so those strings need review by Arabic-speaking lifters before launch.

## Three visual directions share one native-feel kit

Recent design awards point to tone rather than flash. No dedicated strength logger won an Apple Design Award or a Google Play "Best of" between 2024 and 2026. The recognised fitness designs were Gentler Streak, plus the 2025 finalists Train Fitness and Evolve, honoured for inclusivity and a friendly tone ([Apple Developer, ADA 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/); [Apple Newsroom, ADA 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-reveals-winners-of-the-2026-apple-design-awards/)). The platforms themselves moved toward a compact floating bottom bar. Apple's Liquid Glass tab bars "shrink to bring focus to the content" ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)), and Material 3 Expressive introduced a pill-shaped floating toolbar. Google says, citing its own 46 studies, that expressive designs let users spot key elements "up to 4x faster" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/05/material-3-expressive-leak/)). WHOOP shows how a performance UI handles data: an almost entirely black interface, oversized primary numbers, and a semantic palette where "each color has one meaning and repeats across screens" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)). The three directions below are researcher proposals. The hex values and contrast ratios were computed with the WCAG 2.x formula and are not taken from any app's published design system.

|---|---|---|---|

Fonts and icons in the table come from the notes' proposals, with icon licences confirmed as Lucide ISC and Phosphor/Tabler MIT ([DEV: icon licences](https://dev.to/usapopopooon/what-i-didnt-know-about-icon-library-licenses-and-you-might-not-either-30of)). Font licences and Arabic coverage for Barlow, Oswald, Rubik and Readex Pro were not verified and need checking before bundling.

The recommended choice is **B "Ledger" as the structural system, carrying C's tone**. That means hairline set rows, tabular Plex numerals and one Arabic–Latin superfamily, combined with C's supportive copy, a warmer accent on Home and onboarding, and friendly empty states. Three reasons drive this. It is the cheapest to render on low-end Android. It is the only direction whose Arabic headings will match its Latin ones, whereas A's condensed numerals have no Arabic counterpart. And the award evidence says tone matters more than aesthetic energy for a broad, beginner-heavy audience. If the owner wants a darker, gym-floor identity, A is the alternative. It should still keep B's single type family and dense rows, and limit volt to fills.

All three directions should share the same semantic colours and the same native-feel kit. One meaning per colour works across themes: a green check for done (`#3DDC84` dark, `#16803C` light), the accent for the current set, **amber rather than red for skipped** with "Skipped" instead of "Failed", red only for destructive actions, and gold for PRs. Each colour should be paired with an icon so meaning never rests on colour alone. The PWA basics are well documented: `display: standalone`, `viewport-fit=cover` with safe-area insets, separate light and dark `theme-color`, `overscroll-behavior-y: contain` to stop pull-to-refresh, and `user-select: none` only on controls ([web.dev Learn PWA](https://web.dev/learn/pwa/app-design)). Same-document View Transitions now work in Chrome 111+, Safari 18+ and Firefox 144+ ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025)). They are worth using for tab and sheet changes, behind feature detection and `prefers-reduced-motion`. Frosted glass is the one trend to avoid. `backdrop-filter` is the most expensive common CSS effect. One indicative, methodology-free test reported **30–45 FPS** with it ([macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/liquid-glass-performance-comparison/)), and a long-open Mozilla bug ties it to janky scrolling on Android ([Bugzilla 1732817](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1732817)). Google itself uses blur only in system surfaces ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/13/android-16-material-3-expressive-redesign/)). A floating, opaque pill tab bar gets the modern look without that cost.

P0 items address the strongest evidence: retention in the first session, taps per set, and the trust of the sharing flow. P1 items add depth that users praise elsewhere. P2 items are polish.

Two of these choices remain untested hypotheses and should be the first targets for hallway testing with Iraqi users. One is the RTL position of the −/+ steppers. The other is whether a progress chart's time axis should run left to right. Both are cheap to A/B test with two builds and a handful of lifters, and no published source settles either.

## Conclusion

Visual style is the least important of Lift Ledger's decisions. The evidence keeps pointing at things users feel rather than see: whether a repeat set is one tap, whether the first session starts in seconds, whether a missed week produces guilt, and whether a trainer's update arrives without wiping the trainee's history. That reframes the redesign. The workout row and the import preview deserve more design time than the palette. A properly mirrored Arabic interface with stable Western digits can give Lift Ledger an advantage that paid apps have not prioritised.

The encoded, serverless share link is the most underrated asset. Competitors treat sharing as a server feature that needs an account. Lift Ledger's link is the program itself, so it can preview offline, import from a QR code scanned in a basement gym, and travel through WhatsApp as easily as a PDF. If the import screen makes "update vs copy" explicit and the share sheet leads with WhatsApp, trainers become the distribution channel. That is how a free app with no marketing budget could reach the tens of thousands of users the owner expects. The main unknowns are Iraqi digit and terminology preferences, real-world retention for an uninstalled PWA, and RTL chart and stepper conventions. Each can be answered cheaply with a small group of local lifters before launch rather than guessed at.
