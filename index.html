What does RPE mean?

RPE is how hard a set feels on a 1–10 scale, judged by how many more reps you could have done before failing.

Two numbers like 7–8 / 9 mean your early sets at RPE 7–8 and your last set at 9. Reps are a range: when every set reaches the top of the range, add weight next time.

          How this is calculated

          BMR uses Mifflin-St Jeor (weight, height, age and sex). If you enter a body fat %, it's averaged with Katch-McArdle, which uses your lean mass.

          Daily life multiplies BMR to cover a normal day at your activity level, including digesting food.

          Treadmill burn uses the ACSM walking formula from speed, incline and your weight, counting only what you burn above resting, because BMR already covers that. It assumes you don't hold the handrails. Treadmill displays usually show total calories, so they read higher.

          Weight training uses MET values for resistance training (moderate 3.5, hard 5.0). Most of a session is rest between sets, which is why it burns less than cardio.

          Weight change uses about 7,700 kcal per kg of body fat. Water and food in your gut make the scale swing day to day, so judge trends over 2–3 weeks.

          Accuracy: expect about ±10%. If your weight trend over 3–4 weeks doesn't match the estimate, adjust "Daily life" until it does. This is an estimate, not medical advice.
